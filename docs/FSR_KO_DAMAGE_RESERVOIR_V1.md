# FSR KO / Damage Reservoir V1 — Mechanics Specification

Status: shadow/research architecture. This document defines the intended mechanics and trait roles. Numeric calibration constants remain provisional. No production promotion is authorized by this document.

## 1. Purpose

Define the smallest damage-state system needed for the RFS/FSR Monte Carlo engine using the historical studies completed after the V0 checkpoint.

The system should explain:

- why some landed strikes are more damaging than others;
- why high-power fighters score more knockdowns per landed significant strike;
- why some fighters are harder to knock down even when strikes land;
- why some fighters survive much larger accumulated damage loads;
- why a knockdown creates a sharply more dangerous follow-up state;
- why repeated damage can finish a fighter without introducing multiple redundant hidden meters.

The design deliberately avoids separate acute-stress, consciousness, hurt, cumulative-damage, and finish-danger meters unless future evidence demonstrates they are required.

## 2. Active finish-trait ontology

### 2.1 `striking_power`

Role: offensive strike-severity trait.

Historical support:

- higher pre-fight striking power predicts higher knockdown probability;
- higher striking power predicts higher knockdowns per landed significant strike;
- the relationship persists when fighters are compared inside landed-strike exposure strata.

Simulator role:

`striking_power` changes the distribution of damage generated by a landed strike, especially the upper tail. It should not simply multiply every landed strike by a constant amount and should not primarily control strike volume.

### 2.2 `knockdown_resistance`

Role: acute shock tolerance / first-knockdown resistance.

Historical support:

- a leakage-safe prior-history candidate showed monotonic reductions in future knockdown absorption as resistance increased;
- the `striking_power - opponent knockdown_resistance` edge separated knockdown outcomes better than striking power alone;
- the former `chin_resistance` trait did not predict first-knockdown avoidance and must not be used for this role.

Simulator role:

`knockdown_resistance` modifies the probability that a large strike-level reservoir loss causes a knockdown. It does not determine total reservoir capacity.

### 2.3 `damage_durability`

Role: cumulative damage tolerance / reservoir-capacity trait.

Historical support:

- a leakage-safe prior-history durability candidate predicted lower future KO/TKO loss;
- separation became especially strong inside matched realized damage-exposure strata;
- among KD-exposed fighters, higher durability predicted better KO/TKO survival more consistently than the former `chin_resistance` trait;
- the former `damage_resistance` rating was materially weaker and had very little useful numeric spread.

Simulator role:

`damage_durability` determines fighter-specific reservoir capacity and therefore how much accumulated damage a fighter can tolerate before entering severely depleted states.

### 2.4 Retired / inactive finish traits

The following existing traits should not be active simulator parameters in the V1 damage design:

- `chin_resistance` — historical construction primarily reflected post-KD survival and did not generalize to first-KD avoidance;
- `damage_resistance` — weaker than the newly discovered durability signal and not retained as a separate per-strike mitigation trait without additional evidence.

They may remain available in historical artifacts for compatibility/audit purposes until a later schema migration is explicitly approved.

## 3. Dynamic path state

Each fighter path requires only the following damage-state variables:

- `reservoir_capacity`
- `reservoir_current`
- `recent_knockdown` or equivalent short-lived KD vulnerability timer/state

Derived values may be calculated from these without storing parallel meters.

At fight start:

```text
reservoir_current = reservoir_capacity
recent_knockdown = false
```

## 4. Reservoir scale and capacity

### 4.1 Normalized scale

Use a normalized reservoir unit system.

Reference average:

```text
average reservoir capacity = 100 units
```

The number 100 has no direct physiological meaning. It is an internal unit convention only. Multiplying every capacity and every strike-damage value by the same constant would describe the same model.

### 4.2 Fighter-specific capacity

Reservoir size must differ by fighter.

Conceptually:

```text
reservoir_capacity = capacity_map(damage_durability)
```

Requirements for the capacity mapping:

- centered on 100 for an average durability fighter;
- monotonic in `damage_durability`;
- bounded to prevent extreme historical ratings from producing implausible capacities;
- numeric spread to be chosen later from simulation validation, not assumed from the 0-100 durability research score itself.

Do not interpret the durability research score as literal reservoir units.

## 5. Strike damage generation

Every landed significant strike should remove some reservoir.

Conceptual pipeline:

```text
landed significant strike
    -> base severity draw
    -> striking_power modifies severity distribution
    -> contextual modifiers if justified
    -> strike_damage
    -> reservoir_current -= strike_damage
```

### 5.1 Severity distribution

Damage must be stochastic.

Requirements:

- most landed significant strikes produce relatively small reservoir losses;
- a minority produce moderate losses;
- a small upper tail produces very large losses;
- higher `striking_power` primarily increases the probability/magnitude of upper-tail events rather than making every strike uniformly powerful.

This is required to allow both volume damage and isolated bombs.

### 5.2 No separate per-strike damage-resistance trait in V1

The current evidence supports fighter-specific total durability and fighter-specific knockdown resistance, but does not yet justify another independent trait that simply reduces every strike's reservoir damage.

Therefore V1 starts with:

```text
strike_damage = severity_from_attacker_power_and_context
```

while defender durability acts through capacity and defender knockdown resistance acts through KD susceptibility.

A future per-strike mitigation trait may be added only if new evidence shows it adds information beyond durability and KD resistance.

## 6. Reservoir condition

Define:

```text
reservoir_fraction = reservoir_current / reservoir_capacity
```

This is the primary cumulative-damage condition variable.

The simulator may use `reservoir_fraction` to alter vulnerability as the fighter becomes depleted, but the exact curve remains provisional.

Historical evidence showed that prior damaging exposure is associated with greater later knockdown probability, but did not prove that every later strike becomes intrinsically more damaging. Therefore V1 must not hard-code an aggressive strike-damage amplification purely from low reservoir without separate validation.

Recommended initial interpretation:

- low reservoir fraction increases finish/KD susceptibility;
- it does not automatically multiply raw strike damage by a large factor.

## 7. Knockdown mechanics

A knockdown is an acute consequence of a damaging strike, not simply a reservoir threshold crossing.

For each landed strike:

```text
shock_fraction = strike_damage / reservoir_capacity
```

Knockdown probability is a function of at least:

```text
shock_fraction
knockdown_resistance
reservoir_fraction
recent_knockdown state if already vulnerable
```

Conceptually:

```text
p(KD) = KD_model(
    shock_fraction,
    knockdown_resistance,
    reservoir_fraction,
    recent_knockdown
)
```

Important constraints:

- no single hard shock cutoff is required;
- a fresh fighter can be knocked down by a rare extreme strike;
- a depleted fighter can be knocked down by a less extreme strike;
- high `knockdown_resistance` lowers acute KD probability but does not prevent reservoir depletion from the strike itself;
- a fighter can therefore absorb a large reservoir loss without being knocked down.

## 8. Post-knockdown vulnerability

A knockdown creates a short-lived dynamic vulnerability state.

Historical support:

- KO/TKO loss probability rises dramatically after a KD;
- KD rounds contain substantially more head, ground, and total significant-strike damage;
- the association remains strong even after terminal KO/TKO rounds are removed;
- a separate static post-KD-survival trait did not add sufficiently stable information once durability and adversity were controlled.

Therefore the V1 model uses a dynamic state rather than a fourth fighter trait.

Conceptually:

```text
KD
  -> recent_knockdown = true
  -> temporary vulnerability modifier
  -> elevated follow-up danger
```

The vulnerability state may affect:

- probability of another KD;
- probability of KO/TKO stoppage;
- follow-up ground/damaging opportunity;
- effective use of remaining durability.

The exact magnitude and duration are calibration parameters, not new FSR traits.

## 9. KO/TKO pathways

V1 should permit multiple finish paths.

### 9.1 Catastrophic acute finish

```text
extreme strike severity
-> large reservoir loss / shock
-> KD or direct severe finish event
```

A fighter does not need to be near zero reservoir before suffering a catastrophic event.

### 9.2 Accumulation finish

```text
repeated landed strikes
-> reservoir depletion
-> lower reservoir_fraction
-> higher finish susceptibility
-> KD and/or stoppage
```

### 9.3 Post-KD follow-up finish

```text
KD
-> temporary vulnerability
-> follow-up strikes / ground offense
-> additional reservoir depletion
-> KO/TKO
```

A KD should not automatically subtract a second arbitrary block of reservoir. The strike already caused its reservoir loss. The KD changes the subsequent state and finish hazard.

## 10. Reservoir exhaustion

`reservoir_current <= 0` should represent extreme accumulated damage, but V1 should not rely on a deterministic zero-health rule as the only KO/TKO mechanism.

Recommended behavior:

- approaching zero sharply increases finish probability;
- reaching zero should make continued survival extremely unlikely;
- catastrophic acute finishes may occur above zero;
- a fighter may also survive temporarily at low positive reservoir if no decisive finish event is drawn.

## 11. Recovery boundary

Recovery remains part of the future dynamic-state layer.

The damage reservoir may refill under later approved mechanics, potentially using `recovery_ability`, but V1 does not yet define the numeric refill curve.

Any recovery implementation must distinguish:

- restoration of `reservoir_current`;
- unchanged `reservoir_capacity` for that fight unless a separate persistent-injury model is explicitly introduced later.

Do not introduce a second persistent damage ceiling unless historical evidence requires it.

## 12. Interaction with stamina

Damage reservoir and stamina reservoir are separate dynamic resources.

Planned division:

- damage reservoir: accumulated physical damage / finish vulnerability;
- stamina reservoir: expendable work capacity / fatigue.

Future interactions may allow low stamina to affect output, defense, recovery, or damage vulnerability, but damage and stamina should remain separate meters to avoid conflating fatigue with injury.

## 13. Research-backed architecture decisions

The completed historical studies support the following V1 decisions:

1. accumulated damaging exposure is associated with increased later KD probability;
2. KD is a major finish-regime change;
3. KD rounds show substantially elevated damaging offense, including in non-terminal rounds;
4. `striking_power` meaningfully predicts KD production per landed strike;
5. former `chin_resistance` does not measure first-KD resistance;
6. a leakage-safe `knockdown_resistance` candidate does predict first-KD avoidance and improves power-vs-defense matchup separation;
7. a leakage-safe `damage_durability` candidate predicts KO/TKO survival under matched damage exposure;
8. `damage_durability` explains post-KD survival more consistently than former `chin_resistance`;
9. no separate post-KD-survival fighter trait is required for V1.

## 14. V1 active trait summary

```text
striking_power
    -> offensive strike-severity distribution

knockdown_resistance
    -> acute KD susceptibility

damage_durability
    -> fighter-specific reservoir capacity / cumulative survival
```

Dynamic state:

```text
reservoir_capacity
reservoir_current
recent_knockdown
```

## 15. Implementation sequence

When implementation is approved, proceed in this order:

1. implement research-only trait builders for `knockdown_resistance` and `damage_durability` at leakage-safe pre-fight grain;
2. add compatibility aliases / migration handling rather than silently breaking existing FSR schema;
3. implement isolated reservoir state and strike-damage draws in the static MC shadow engine;
4. implement KD probability from shock + KD resistance + reservoir condition;
5. implement temporary post-KD vulnerability;
6. implement KO/TKO finish hazard;
7. audit path behavior and historical aggregate reproduction;
8. only then tune numeric constants;
9. keep all changes shadow-only until explicit promotion approval.

## 16. Non-goals for V1

Do not add without new evidence:

- acute-stress meter;
- consciousness meter;
- separate hurt meter;
- separate cumulative-damage meter;
- static post-KD-survival trait;
- generic per-strike damage-resistance trait;
- deterministic hard KD threshold;
- deterministic reservoir-zero-only finish rule.
